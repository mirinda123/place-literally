These 34 targets are Chinese province-level place labels, including the names
of Beijing, Hong Kong, Macau and Taiwan already present in ES. For a Hanzi
name, prefer a locally appropriate Chinese language tag (zh, zh-Hans, zh-Hant,
or a genuinely local vernacular such as yue for Hong Kong or wuu for Shanghai).
Do not select a tag from a geographically unrelated Chinese variety simply
because it has a shorter spelling. The full official name may include 省, 市,
自治区 or 特别行政区; that is acceptable as literal_name. Explain the meaningful
name stem and administrative suffix naturally in the translations.

Research the documented naming history before translating individual Hanzi.
Some province names join the names of earlier prefectures or cities: 安徽省
comes from 安庆 and 徽州; 江苏省 from 江宁 and 苏州. For such a name, do not call
安徽 "peaceful and beautiful" or 江苏 "river awakening" based only on the
characters. Translate the supported naming chain, retaining proper names
whose own deeper lexical origin is unverified. Trace an older component's
lexical meaning only when a reliable source supports it. For directly
descriptive names, keep their geographic referent and direction (for example,
河北 is north of the relevant river, not merely "north"). Do not confuse
the origin of a current regional label with a modern literal reading of its
characters. Put evidence and uncertainty in note, never in translation values.
